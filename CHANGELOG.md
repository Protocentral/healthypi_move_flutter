# Release notes — HealthyPi Move app

App ID `com.protocentral.move`. Available on
[Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.protocentral.move)
and the App Store.

---

## 3.0.x — the v3 release

Version 3 is a full rebuild of the HealthyPi Move companion app: a new interface,
a new way of getting data off the watch, and a real recordings library. Everything
still runs entirely on your phone — there is no account, no cloud, and no backend.

### Please read before updating

**Version 3 requires version 3 firmware on your watch.** The old data-transfer
protocol has been removed, so a v3 app and a pre-v3 watch cannot talk to each
other, and neither can a pre-v3 app and a v3 watch. Update the app, then use the
built-in firmware updater on the **Device** tab to bring the watch to a matching
release. If your watch is on older firmware, the app will say so and offer the
update directly.

For the same reason, version 3 is a **required update** for anyone on 2.x — the
app will ask you to update before it will run.

Minimum platform versions: **iOS 13.1** and **Android 5.0 (API 21)**.

### New in 3.0.0

#### Redesigned from scratch

- Four tabs — **Home**, **Trends**, **Live**, **Device** — replacing the old
  screen-per-task layout.
- A dark, high-contrast theme with a distinct colour per metric, so heart rate,
  SpO₂, temperature and activity are recognisable at a glance.
- Proper tablet and large-screen support: the navigation bar becomes a side rail,
  the home screen lays out in columns, and the Live view can show two signals at
  once.
- Redesigned onboarding: scan, pair, and you're done.

#### A new, much faster sync

- Health data now transfers over the watch's Health Store, which the watch keeps
  filled continuously rather than assembling on request.
- Sync is **resumable** — an interrupted transfer picks up where it stopped
  instead of starting over — **cancellable**, and fetches the newest samples
  first, so the numbers you care about appear before the backlog finishes.
- Repeat syncs only fetch what's new. The old behaviour of re-downloading the
  same recent window on every tap is gone.
- Data is only acknowledged to the watch after it has been written to your phone
  and checksum-verified, so a failed sync can't lose readings.

#### Trends worth looking at

- Hourly and daily summaries with a rolling 30-day baseline, so a value is shown
  against *your* usual, not a generic chart.
- Day, week, month and 6-month ranges, each with its own axis labelling.
- Tap any metric for a detail view with its own history and statistics.
- **Continuous HRV** and a stress score derived from it. While the app is still
  learning your baseline it says so rather than showing a meaningless zero.
- Steps are now derived correctly from the watch's cumulative counter.

#### Recordings library

- Episodic captures — ECG, PPG, GSR/EDA, HRV and motion — are listed as soon as
  the watch reports them, and downloaded only when you tap one, so browsing the
  library costs nothing.
- Every downloaded recording is checksum-verified.
- Preview a recording on screen, then export it as CSV to share or analyse
  elsewhere.

#### Blood pressure

- A blood-pressure screen showing an estimated range and how it is trending
  against your own usual readings.
- Guided calibration runs from the app, with live contact and progress feedback.
- These are wellness estimates from an optical sensor. They are not a diagnosis,
  not a medical-device measurement, and are shown as a range and a trend rather
  than a single number for exactly that reason.

#### Firmware updates

- The updater has been rebuilt and handles the current bootloader correctly,
  including the two-part update that newer watches need.
- The app checks the update package against your watch before sending anything,
  so a mismatched file fails immediately instead of part-way through a flash.

#### Live view

- Live ECG, PPG, heart rate, SpO₂ and temperature streaming, redesigned, with
  a two-signal layout on tablets.

#### Your data stays yours

- **Delete all data on this phone** in Settings — removes every stored reading
  and recording and resets the sync position.
- CSV export for recordings.
- Test and demonstration data generated by watch firmware is stored but never
  charted, summarised or exported. If anything synthetic is ever on screen, the
  app says so with a banner.

### 3.0.1

- **Firmware updates now complete successfully.** Updates could run to 100% and
  then fail on the watch. Four separate faults were behind it — how the new image
  was staged, how it was identified to the bootloader, a connection that had gone
  stale while the screen sat open, and a misreported result. All fixed, with a
  clear error if a package genuinely can't be installed.
- **iOS: no more camera permission prompt.** The file picker used for imports
  pulled in a camera dependency it never needed. The app now uses the document
  picker only, and no longer asks for camera access.
- Version 3 is enforced as a required update for 2.x users, since 2.x cannot read
  a v3 watch at all.

---

### Unreleased

- **Automatic sync.** The app now syncs on launch, when you bring it back to the
  foreground, and periodically while it's open — no longer only when you tap
  **Sync now**. It won't interrupt a live stream or an update in progress, and
  it's throttled so repeated app switching doesn't hammer the watch. There's a
  toggle in Settings, and the manual **Sync now** buttons are unchanged. Sync
  runs while the app is open; iOS does not allow this kind of Bluetooth work in
  the background.
- **Firmware updates announce themselves.** Availability is now checked in the
  background and shown on the Device tab, instead of only appearing if you
  happened to open the update screen. The Device tab also shows the watch's
  firmware version, which had been blank.
- **Update compatibility gate.** A firmware release can now declare the minimum
  app version it needs, and the app refuses to install one it's too old for.
  Since the bootloader cannot roll back, this prevents ending up with a watch the
  app can't talk to.
- **ECG recordings were missing from the library.** The signal codes were off by
  one, so ECG recordings never appeared at all, GSR sessions were labelled PPG,
  ECG sessions were labelled GSR, and motion recordings fell through to "other".
  Existing recordings on your phone are relabelled correctly — nothing needs to
  be re-downloaded. An ECG filter has been added alongside the others.
- **PPG recordings drew as a flat line.** A brief settling transient at the start
  of a capture took over the whole vertical axis. Recordings now auto-scale around
  the signal itself, and CSV export includes a detrended column alongside the raw
  one.
- **The share button on Trends now works** — it exports the range you're looking
  at as CSV. It previously did nothing at all.
- **Month and 6M showed the same data.** 6M is now a genuine weekly-binned
  six-month series, Month is a rolling 30 days, and each range labels its axis in
  its own units instead of using weekday names throughout.
- **Trend statistics follow the selected range** instead of always showing today.
- A metric with exactly one reading in range now draws as a point rather than
  nothing at all.
- **Detail screens no longer show "--" for a metric you measured a few days ago.**
  This mostly affected SpO₂, which is a spot check: Home showed a value while the
  detail view claimed there was none.
- **Delete all data now deletes the downloaded recording files too**, not just
  the entries pointing at them, and sweeps up files orphaned by earlier versions.
  Open screens refresh immediately instead of continuing to show deleted data.
- **Live signals start when you connect.** Streams were only ever stopped, never
  started, on connection, and re-tapping the signal you were already on did
  nothing — which made ECG, the default, the hardest one to recover. Failures to
  subscribe are now reported instead of showing an empty grid under a "connected"
  badge, and an idle ECG or GSR trace explains that the capture has to be started
  on the watch.
