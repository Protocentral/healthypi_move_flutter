Health-Store Sync Redesign (HS-2)

Why the app sync takes ~0.7 MB and minutes of flash thrashing to move half a megabyte — and how to make it 8× smaller, O(1) to read, and gain continuous HRV along the way. Detail: docs/HS_SYNC_REDESIGN_PLAN.md

01The headline

Three independent problems, plus one large missed opportunity. Only one of them is the read path everybody was looking at.

The one that matters most is not a performance bug

Retention is ~1.2 days because we store 46,000 samples/day. If the user doesn't open the app for two days, that data is gone forever. This is a correctness bug hiding behind a performance bug — and it's happening while we use 0.9% of a 112 MiB flash volume.

02Root cause: we store the wrong things, far too often

Skin temperature is sampled every 5 seconds. Skin temp moves over minutes — that's roughly 300× oversampled for the physiology. Heart rate every 3 seconds is similarly beyond anything a trend consumer uses. Garmin-class devices bucket the day into 15-minute epochs; Apple stores HR about every 5 minutes at rest. Nobody ships 3-second HR to the phone as the durable record.

Nothing actually consumes it

The live HR tile reads zbus, not the store. Beat-level fidelity, where it genuinely matters, already lives in the separate Record tier (ECG strips, R-R). The 3-second HR samples exist only to be written to flash and shipped over BLE.

03Root cause: every page rescans the log from the start

hpi_hs_read_since() has no seq→position map and no fs_seek. To serve any page it opens the oldest segment, reads every record, throws away everything at or below the cursor, and stops once it has collected 40.

Serving one 40-sample page at since = 31,000:

012345678910…606162636465666768
opened, fully read, entirely discarded the segment we actually wanted

69 files opened and read to return 720 bytes. And all ~80 files sit in one flat directory, so every fs_open is also a linear metadata walk.

pages       = 35,088 / 40                 = 877
record reads = Σ i·40  for i = 1..877      ≈ 15,400,000
flash read   = 15.4M × 18 B                ≈ 277 MB   ← to move 630 KB
file opens   ≈ 34,000                      ← for 78 files

The information needed to jump straight to the right byte is already implicit in the layout — seq is monotonic, segments are contiguous and near-fixed-size. It's simply never recorded or exploited.

04Root cause: two background jobs churn flash forever

Neither has anything to do with sync. They burn power whether or not anyone ever opens the app.

meta is rewritten every 10 seconds

hs_persist_meta() → hs_overwrite() does unlink + create + write on every flush that wrote data. At a 10-second flush cadence that's ~8,640 file rewrites per day, forever. LittleFS wear and QSPI power, for a handful of bytes of cursor state.

SUMMARY rescans the entire log ~8× every 5 minutes

hpi_hs_summary() calls hs_durable_scan() about eight times (HR today, SpO₂, temp today, temp 7-day baseline, HRV today, HRV baseline, HR histogram…). Each is a full scan of every segment. That's roughly 5 MB of flash reads every 5 minutes.

05The missed opportunity: we already have R-R intervals

The MAX32664C emits R-R intervals — and nothing reads them

The wrist hub computes rtor and rtor_confidence in its algo FIFO. The driver already parses them (max32664c_async.c:335-340). They're already in the struct (hpi_ppg_wr_data_t.rtor). Then they are dropped on the floor.

Today HRV only comes from ecg_sensor_sample.rtor, gated on is_hrv_eval_active — ECG only, and only when the user manually sits down and takes a reading.

The PPG is already running. We're already paying its power bill to produce that 3-second HR. R-R falls out of the same FIFO for free. We are keeping the sensor's least valuable output and discarding its most valuable one.

What R-R unlocks

  • Continuous / overnight HRV — the metric users actually care about. Whoop, Oura and Garmin all derive it from PPG, not ECG.
  • Stress — ours is EDA-only today. HRV + EDA together is substantially stronger.
  • Recovery / readiness — impossible today.
  • Better resting HR — R-R gives a cleaner estimate than sampled HR.

How to store it — not as raw samples

Raw R-R at 60 bpm is 86,400 intervals/day — that would be worse than what we have now. Instead:

1 · Gate hard
Accept an interval only when confidence is high, on-skin, still (scd_state + activity_class, both already decoded), and 300–1500 ms. Motion destroys PRV.
→
2 · 5-min windows
Compute RMSSD, SDNN, mean RR and coverage % on-device. RMSSD is the robust short-window standard and the best fit for PPG.
→
3 · Store epochs
~300–800 records/day, only during valid windows. Raw R-R goes to the Record tier nightly if wanted (~57 KB/night).

Coverage % is not optional

Without it the app cannot distinguish a clean 5-minute window from a noisy one, and will happily plot motion artefacts as a physiological trend. This is the difference between a trustworthy HRV chart and a garbage one.

We emit min and max alongside the mean, not the mean alone. That costs 3 records/minute for HR instead of 1 — 8× instead of 12× — and it is worth every byte. A 10-second spike to 150 bpm reads as 150 today; as a minute-mean it would collapse to ~110. Systematically under-reporting peak heart rate on a health device is not an acceptable trade.

Pick the right statistic per signal — don't apply mean/min/max uniformly

Symmetry is the wrong instinct. Blanket-applying HR's mean/min/max to steps or skin temp would be redundant at best and meaningless at worst.

The governing rule

You only need within-epoch min/max when the signal varies significantly inside the epoch.

Signal kindExampleRight statisticWhy
Spiky — peaks are realHR mean + min + max Can go 70→150→70 inside one minute. The mean destroys the peak.
Cumulative counterSTEPS, ENERGY last value in window Monotonic within a day, so max ≡ last and min ≡ first (both redundant), and the mean of a monotonic ramp is meaningless — it's the midpoint of the ramp, not "average steps".
Slow continuous — the level mattersSKIN_TEMP mean + count Moves over minutes, so within a 5-min window the spread is a fraction of a degree: the mean faithfully represents the whole window. A nightly min or daily max is recoverable from the series of means — within-epoch extremes aren't needed.
Sparse eventBP, SpO₂ spot keep raw — no epoch Already only tens per day.
Variability metricHRV_* window + coverage The window is the measurement.

Skin temp gets no min/max — and it's not just redundancy, it would be harmful

A within-epoch temp max would preferentially capture artifacts — contact pressure, sun, the watch coming off — not physiology. Nobody asks "what was my highest skin temperature today?", and if they did, the honest answer would be noise.

The product uses deviation from baseline, which wants a robust central estimate, not an extreme. Shipping a TEMP_MAX that looks meaningful but is artifact-dominated is worse than not shipping one. Cost isn't the objection (576 extra records/day is nothing) — meaning is.

⚠️ The midnight step reset must force-flush the epoch

STEPS is cumulative and resets to 0 at local midnight. Today every change is recorded, so the reset appears as 8000 → 0 and the day's final total survives.

Naively rate-limiting to 1/min would silently lose it. If the reset falls inside a window and we emit only the last value, we emit 0 — and the day's final step count is gone forever.

The aggregator must force-flush on any decrease, emitting the pre-reset peak before the counter rolls. This is a bug we would be introducing, and it exists only because we are rate-limiting.

Validate the epoch lengths — don't trust the reasoning above

1 min for HR and 5 min for temp rest on an assumption about how much each signal moves within a window. Measure it: instrument the aggregator to log within-epoch spread (max − min) over a day of real wear. If within-minute HR spread is routinely small, HR could go to a 5-minute epoch and save another 5×. If within-5-minute temp spread turns out large, my "temp is slow" assumption is wrong and temp needs min/max after all. Cheap experiment; it either confirms the design or catches a lossy one.

07Fixing the read path: arithmetic, not search

Roll segments at a fixed record count instead of "≥ 8192 bytes after a variable-size batch". Then position is pure arithmetic — no index file, no RAM index, nothing to persist or rebuild:

seg_idx = (seq - 1) / HS_SEG_RECORDS;                    // which file
offset  = ((seq - 1) % HS_SEG_RECORDS) * 18;             // where in it

The same page at since = 31,000, after the fix:

012345678910…606162636465666768

One fs_open, one fs_seek, one fs_read. 277 MB → the payload size. 34,000 opens → one per page.

Subtle trap worth writing down

hs_flush() calls hpi_hs_read_since() as its own source of unflushed samples. Any change to the read path must not let the flusher re-read and re-append what it already wrote. It can't — the scan only yields seq > since_seq, and segments only ever hold <= s_flushed_seq — but the invariant is non-obvious and must stay commented. This nearly bit the cold-ring fix.

08Transfer: whole segments, not scattered pages

A rolled segment is immutable, fixed-size, and self-decodable (just packed records). It's already the right unit of transfer — it just isn't addressable as one.

Live tail
SYNC — small pages from the RAM ring. Hot, no flash I/O at all. Unchanged.
+
Backlog
SEGS {idx, off, len} — fetch whole segments. One fs_open per segment, sequential reads, handle cached across chunks.
=
Client
Caches by segment index. A rolled segment never changes, so "already have it? skip" is safe forever.

This is the old per-day-file model — "send the whole file, skip the ones you already have" — but keyed on segment index instead of date. Same benefit, without needing RTC-aligned day boundaries or breaking the seq cursor. And the app already has this exact code path: it's how RECORDS get works.

Full-sync file opens: ~34,000 → ~10.

12What P1 actually breaks

An earlier draft of this plan claimed P1 needed no app change. That was wrong. The distinction matters, because this is the kind of error that surfaces as a user-visible regression months later.

ClaimVerdict
Wire format unchanged — 18 B, same fields, same commands, same cursor✅ true
App keeps working unmodified — parses, syncs, dedups, doesn't crash✅ true
No schema bump (new type ids are additive)✅ true
App stays correct unmodified❌ FALSE

Peak heart rate would silently regress

An HR sample changes meaning: from "reading at this instant" to "mean over the minute ending here". Anything the app derives from the raw series — daily max, percentiles, HR zones — shifts downward and systematically. A 10-second spike to 150 bpm is 150 today and ~110 as a minute-mean.

"Nothing breaks" is not the same as "nothing changes". The app keeps running; it stops being right.

The fix — pick one

  • (a) Read HR min/max from SUMMARY — hr_min / hr_avg / hr_max already exist in struct hpi_hs_summary and are computed on-device from the min/max epochs, so they stay exact. If the app already uses SUMMARY for its cards this may be zero work — as long as it does not recompute peaks from the mean series.
  • (b) Adopt the new HR_MIN / HR_MAX type ids — additive, harmlessly skipped by old builds, and lets the app draw a proper band around the mean.

Also changing under P1

  • Density drops ~20× (3 s → 60 s). Any "no sample in 30 s ⇒ stale/disconnected" logic will misfire.
  • ts_utc on an epoch = end of the window, not an instant of measurement.
  • STEPS is unchanged — still cumulative. An earlier draft proposed per-minute deltas; that is a hard semantic break (the app would plot deltas as a running total) and has been dropped. We simply rate-limit recording to ≤1/min.

Still open: does anything consume 3-second HR?

As far as I can trace, no — the live tile reads zbus, not the store, and beat-level data already lives in the Record tier. But this needs a yes/no from the app team before P1 lands. If they have built on raw density, P1 keeps a high-resolution window for the last N hours and epochs everything older.
